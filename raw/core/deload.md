## Sources

### Source 1
- Title: "Scientific Principles of Strength Training"
- Author(s): Mike Israetel, James Hoffmann, Chad Wesley Smith
- Year: 2015
- Publisher: Renaissance Periodization
- URL: https://rpstrength.com/products/scientific-principles-of-strength-training
- Credibility: expert_consensus
- Key facts extracted:
  - Deload = planned reduction in training stress to allow accumulated fatigue to dissipate
  - The SRA model requires that fatigue be cleared before the next adaptation stimulus
    can be expressed; deloads force this clearance
  - Two primary deload mechanisms:
    1. Volume deload: keep intensity high, reduce sets/volume by ~40–60%
    2. Intensity deload: keep volume, reduce load to ~50–70% of normal working weights
  - A third option: complete rest (rarely optimal; skill loss and psychological disruption)
  - Recommended deload frequency: every 4–8 weeks for most intermediate lifters;
    advanced lifters may need more frequent deloads (3–4 weeks)
  - Deload week RPE target: ~6–7 (should feel easy)
  - Accumulation of fatigue masking fitness: after a deload, 1RM often increases because
    fitness was there but hidden by fatigue
- Limitations / caveats:
  - Frequency recommendations based on coaching synthesis, not large RCTs
  - Individual variability is high; some lifters recover faster than others

### Source 2
- Title: "Fatigue Management in Strength Training" (RP article)
- Author(s): Mike Israetel
- Year: 2019
- URL: https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/fatigue-management
- Credibility: practitioner
- Key facts extracted:
  - Fatigue indicators that signal a deload is needed:
    - Performance decline over 2+ consecutive sessions
    - Persistent joint pain or tenderness
    - Motivational loss and dread of training
    - Sleep quality degradation
  - "Reactive deloads" (triggered by indicators) vs. "planned deloads" (scheduled)
  - Both are valid; planned deloads are more consistent for intermediate lifters;
    reactive deloads suit advanced athletes who can self-monitor accurately
- Limitations / caveats:
  - These are coaching heuristics; threshold values are not precisely quantified

### Source 3
- Title: "Tapering and Peaking for Optimal Performance" (NSCA)
- Author(s): Murach, Bagley
- Year: 2015
- Journal: Strength & Conditioning Journal
- DOI: 10.1519/SSC.0000000000000138
- Credibility: expert_consensus (review)
- Key facts extracted:
  - Tapering (sport-science term for deload/peaking) reduces volume by 40–60%
    while maintaining or slightly reducing intensity
  - Duration: 1–4 weeks typically; 1–2 weeks for strength sports
  - Performance improvements of 2–8% observed post-taper in strength athletes
  - Maintains training frequency during taper to preserve motor patterns
- Limitations / caveats:
  - Most taper research in endurance sports; strength sport taper evidence thinner
