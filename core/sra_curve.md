# SRA Curve

The SRA (Stimulus–Recovery–Adaptation) curve describes the fatigue, recovery, and supercompensation a body goes through after a single training stimulus.

## Core Definition

Training lowers functional performance in the moment (fatigue), which then rebounds during recovery and, in the supercompensation phase, rises above the original baseline. The best time for the next session falls at the peak of supercompensation — not while fatigue is still present, and not after supercompensation has decayed back to baseline.

## Three Phases

1. **Stimulus** — training causes muscular microtrauma and metabolic stress; performance temporarily drops
2. **Recovery** — nutrition and sleep drive repair; performance rebounds
3. **Adaptation** — supercompensation sets a new baseline slightly above the previous one

## Curve Length and Training Volume

The larger the stimulus, the longer recovery takes. A high-volume session for a large muscle group can have an SRA cycle of 48–72 hours or more, while a small muscle group or low-volume stimulus may need only 24 hours. This directly informs training frequency design.

## How Systems Differ

Each system arranges different stimulus density and recovery intervals to suit its goals. Those differences are recorded in each system's `index.md`; this entry defines only the principle itself.
