# Brian Shaw Off-Season Training Structure

Brian Shaw is a four-time World's Strongest Man champion (2011, 2013, 2015, 2016) and one of the most documented professional strongman athletes in the sport's history. His off-season training philosophy represents a mature professional framework that prioritizes **absolute strength accumulation at sub-maximal event specificity**: the off-season exists to build a higher strength ceiling for the next competition prep block, not to simulate competitions.

Shaw's system is conjugate-inspired in that it separates maximum effort work from dynamic/volume work across the week, but it does not follow strict Westside percentages or rotation protocols. The system is based on documented training logs from his YouTube channel and competition preparation content from 2011–2020.

The central principle is **progressive overload on absolute strength movements across a full off-season macrocycle (typically 6–8 months between competition cycles)**. Maximum effort days push the training ceiling; volume days accumulate structural hypertrophy and posterior-chain development. Event work is maintenance-level only — enough to preserve the neural patterns, not enough to generate competition-level fatigue.

## Who This Is For

- Elite advanced strongman athletes competing at national or international level
- Athletes in the off-season period between two competition cycles (typically 6–8 months)
- Athletes who can sustain 4 days/week of high-intensity training with professional recovery support
- Not appropriate for: athletes in competition prep, intermediate athletes without established event proficiency

## Weekly Schedule

| Day | Focus |
|-----|-------|
| Monday | ME Deadlift + ME Squat |
| Tuesday | ME Overhead + Upper back / Trap work |
| Wednesday | Accessory (good morning, posterior chain) |
| Thursday | Volume Squat + Events (stones, yoke, tire) |
| Friday | Volume Overhead + Event (log, farmers) |

## Maximum Effort (ME) Day Structure

| Component | Protocol |
|-----------|----------|
| ME Movement | Work up to heavy 3–5 reps (not a true 1RM) |
| Back-off sets | 3 × 5 at 75–80% of day's max |
| Supplemental | 2–3 accessory movements at 3 × 8–12 |

## Key Rules

- **Off-season is not competition prep.** No event maxes, no taper, no intensity peaks. The purpose is strength accumulation, not performance expression. Attempting competition-weight event maxes in the off-season creates unnecessary injury risk without performance benefit.
- **Trap development is non-negotiable.** Heavy shrug volume is as important as the primary strength lifts. The upper trapezius is the primary structural limit of yoke and stone performance at the elite level.
- **Maximum effort work stops at 5 reps.** Working to a true 1RM in the off-season is unnecessary — a heavy 3–5 rep set generates sufficient neural demand while reducing injury risk from maximal loads on tired tissue.

> Transition to `strongman_competition_prep` when a specific contest date is identified (typically 16 weeks out).
