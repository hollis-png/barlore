# Eddie Hall Training Structure

Eddie Hall, the 2017 World's Strongest Man and holder of the first-ever 500 kg deadlift world record (2016), represents the extreme end of strongman training philosophy: **maximum intensity, maximum frequency, professional recovery infrastructure**. His training structure is not a program that intermediate or even most advanced athletes can replicate — it requires professional-grade recovery support (cryotherapy, massage therapy, sleep management, nutritional support) and is documented here as a reference for understanding the apex of the sport.

Hall's central premise was that elite strongman athletes are held back not by lack of training intensity but by inability to recover from it. His solution was to invest in recovery infrastructure equal to his training demands, allowing near-daily heavy sessions without accumulating the overreaching that limits most athletes.

The training structure is notable for two departures from conventional strongman periodization:

1. **No maintenance phases.** Events are trained at competition weight year-round, not periodized to lighter loads in the off-season.
2. **Deadlift as the primary identity lift.** The deadlift session is the anchor of every training week; all other sessions are structured around protecting recovery for the deadlift.

## Caution: Infrastructure Requirements

Hall's training structure requires:
- Professional massage therapy: 3–5 sessions per week
- Cryotherapy or ice bath access daily
- ≥ 9 hours sleep per night (documented)
- Caloric intake of 10,000+ kcal/day to support training volume at his body weight (190 kg)
- Full-time professional athlete status (no other employment)

Attempting this training volume and intensity without the recovery infrastructure produces overreaching within 4–6 weeks in most athletes.

## Who This Is For

- Professional and elite national/international strongman competitors
- Athletes with access to professional recovery support
- Athletes with 4+ years of competitive strongman experience
- Reference material for coaches designing elite-level programs

## 5-Day Weekly Schedule

| Day | Primary Focus |
|-----|---------------|
| Monday | Deadlift (heavy) + Rows |
| Tuesday | Back Squat + Overhead Press + Floor Press |
| Wednesday | Log Press (heavy) + Stones + Tire Flip + Rows |
| Thursday | Deadlift (volume/event) + Farmer Carry + Yoke |
| Friday | Squat (volume) + Overhead Press |
| Saturday | Log (max) + Stones + Accessory |

## Key Rules

- **Deadlift is non-negotiable.** Every training week contains two deadlift sessions regardless of competition proximity. Hall considered this the defining characteristic of his system.
- **Recovery is the training.** The training sessions are the stimulus; the recovery infrastructure is what makes the adaptation possible. Without equivalent recovery, reduce frequency before reducing intensity.
- **Events are never maintenance loads.** If competition weight feels manageable, it is time to increase it. Hall did not accept sub-competition loading as training stimulus at the elite level.

> This structure is not appropriate for competition prep without significant modification. For periodized competition preparation, see `strongman_competition_prep`.
