# CrossFit Aerobic Base Builder

Most CrossFit athletes plateau because they undertrain their aerobic system while overemphasizing high-intensity glycolytic work. The paradox is that the aerobic engine is the recovery mechanism for all other energy systems — a stronger oxidative base means faster phosphocreatine resynthesis between WOD rounds, faster lactate clearance between metcon efforts, and sustained power output across long workouts. This 8-week program temporarily deprioritizes strength PR chasing and gymnastics skill progression in favor of systematically building the aerobic engine.

## Who This Is For

- CrossFit intermediate athletes who breathe hard through all WODs regardless of length (aerobic engine is the limiter, not strength)
- Athletes who consistently underperform on workouts >10 minutes relative to their performance on short sprint WODs
- Athletes who have completed a 6–12 month base CrossFit program and identified aerobic capacity as their primary weakness
- Not for: athletes whose limiter is clearly strength (back squat <1.5× BW) or gymnastics (no kipping pull-up) — fix those first

## The Zone 2 Rationale

Zone 2 is defined as the maximum intensity at which an athlete can sustain nasal breathing, or approximately 60–70% of maximum heart rate (RPE 2–3 of 10). At this intensity:
- Mitochondrial biogenesis is maximized (PGC-1α signaling)
- Type I slow-twitch fibers are selectively trained for fat oxidation
- Lactate is produced and cleared at roughly equal rates (lactate ≈ 1.5–2.0 mmol/L)
- No meaningful CNS or glycogen demand accumulates

Consistent Zone 2 training for 6–8 weeks produces measurable increases in lactate threshold and VO₂max — the physiological substrate that determines how hard an athlete can work for 10+ minutes.

## Weekly Schedule

| Day | Session Type | Duration/Volume |
|-----|-------------|----------------|
| Monday | Zone 2 Row | 25–35 min |
| Tuesday | Strength maintenance (squat + deadlift) + Zone 2 Bike | 30 min bike |
| Wednesday | Threshold intervals (row or bike) | 4–6 × 4–6 min at threshold |
| Thursday | Zone 2 Row or Bike | 30–40 min |
| Friday | Combined WOD (aerobic pace, not sprint) | 20–25 min at Zone 2–3 |
| Saturday/Sunday | Rest or optional easy run (Zone 1, truly easy) | ≤30 min |

## 8-Week Volume Progression

| Week | Total Aerobic Volume | Threshold Work | Character |
|------|---------------------|---------------|-----------|
| 1 | 60 min | None (baseline) | Establish pace |
| 2 | 80 min | 2 × 10 min at threshold | Build base |
| 3 | 100 min | 2 × 12 min | Load week |
| 4 | 90 min | 2 × 8 min | Recovery week |
| 5 | 120 min | 3 × 10 min | Build 2 |
| 6 | 140 min | 3 × 12 min | Peak volume |
| 7 | 120 min | 2 × 10 min | Reduce |
| 8 | 80 min | None (taper) | Test week |

## Threshold Intervals

Threshold pace is 15–20% harder than Zone 2, approximately RPE 6–7 of 10:
- **Row**: ~2:03–2:10/500m for male intermediate
- **Bike**: 250–280W for male intermediate
- Structure: work interval → equal rest → repeat; e.g., 4×6 min / 6 min rest

## End-of-Program Benchmark Tests

Test the following before (Week 1) and after (Week 8) to measure aerobic adaptation:

| Test | Improvement Indicator |
|------|----------------------|
| 2K Row | Time improvement ≥15 sec |
| 5K Run | Time improvement ≥45 sec |
| Cindy (20 min AMRAP) | +2–3 rounds |
| 5-round metcon (e.g., 5×400m row + 10 pull-ups) | Time improvement ≥60 sec |
