# California Strength Daily Training Program

Developed by Dave Spitz at California Strength in the 2000s–2010s, this program represents a modern systematic evolution of Olympic lifting design. Its key innovation is structuring the intensity of daily maximum training within a fatigue-management framework — unlike the pure Bulgarian Method, Cal Strength integrates extensive general physical preparation (GPP) complexes and a structured weekly pattern of heavy and technical days.

The program is organized around a **daily undulating periodization** model: heavy classic lift days (MWF) alternate with technical recovery days (Tu/Th), with Saturday dedicated to maximal squatting volume ("Squaturday"). This structure allows the nervous system to express near-maximal force on heavy days while accumulating technical volume and accessory work on lighter days.

A distinguishing feature is the **mandatory accessory complex** system:
- **Complex A** (upper body structural balance): push press + bent-over rows, supersetted
- **Complex B** (posterior chain integrity): Romanian deadlift + hyperextensions, supersetted

These complexes address the anterior-posterior muscle imbalances and rotator cuff vulnerabilities that develop in athletes who perform nothing but Olympic lifting at high frequency.

## Who This Is For

- Advanced weightlifters who have outgrown weekly periodization models
- Athletes who can train 6 days/week with access to proper recovery (sleep, nutrition, soft tissue)
- Athletes with established technical proficiency in both competition lifts under 85%+ loads
- Not appropriate for: intermediate athletes, athletes with shoulder impingement or patellar tendon issues

## Weekly Schedule

| Day | Session Type | Primary Focus |
|-----|-------------|---------------|
| Monday | Heavy | Snatch + C&J daily max; Complex A |
| Tuesday | Technical | Power variations; Complex A + B |
| Wednesday | Heavy | Snatch + C&J daily max; Complex A |
| Thursday | Technical | Block work + complexes; Complex B |
| Friday | Heavy | Snatch + C&J daily max; Complex A |
| Saturday | Squaturday | 5RM back squat + drop sets |

## Heavy Day Structure (Mon / Wed / Fri)

| Exercise | Protocol | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Snatch | Daily max → 2–3 back-off doubles @85–90% | "Red Zone" max |
| Clean & Jerk | Daily max → 2–3 back-off doubles @85–90% | |
| Front Squat | 3 × 3 | @85–90% front squat 1RM |
| Snatch Pull or Clean Pull | 4 × 3 | 100–120% (phase-dependent) |
| Complex A | 4 rounds: Push Press + Bent-Over Row | Supersetted, moderate load |

## Technical Day Structure (Tue / Thu)

| Exercise | Protocol | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Power Snatch or Hang Snatch | 4 × 2–3 | @70–80%; positional work |
| Clean Complex (clean + FS + jerk from rack) | 4 × 1+1+1 | Pause in rack before jerk |
| Complex A | 3 rounds: Push Press + Bent-Over Row | Light; structural maintenance |
| Complex B | 3 rounds: Romanian Deadlift + Hyperextension | Posterior chain |

## Saturday — Squaturday

| Exercise | Protocol |
|----------|----------|
| Back Squat | Build to 5RM |
| Back Squat drop sets | 2 × 5 at 10% below 5RM |

## Key Rules

- **Back-off doubles are mandatory.** After every daily max, the back-off sets are not optional — they accumulate high-quality volume in a potentiated state while managing systemic fatigue.
- **Complex A and B are not optional accessories.** They exist to prevent the structural imbalances that cause injury in daily-max systems. Skipping them to save time erodes the structural foundation that protects the shoulder and posterior chain.
- **The daily max is bounded by technique, not by ambition.** A lift requiring any positional compromise is not the daily max.
