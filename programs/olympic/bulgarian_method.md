# Bulgarian Method

Developed by Ivan Abadjiev as head coach of the Bulgarian national weightlifting team beginning in the 1970s, the Bulgarian Method is the most radical application of the specificity principle in strength athletics. Abadjiev eliminated all non-competition movements, arguing that any exercise not identical to the snatch, clean & jerk, or front squat introduces non-specific fatigue that competes with adaptation in those movements. The back squat was famously removed because the slightly altered back angle does not perfectly match the receiving position of the clean.

The system operates entirely through **daily maximum autoregulation**: no percentages, no fixed sets-and-reps schemes. Each session the athlete determines the day's ceiling through feel, works up to it, then continues with back-off singles. The high-frequency exposure — up to 12 sessions per week at full development — produces rapid CNS adaptation and motor pattern refinement.

## Who This Is For

- Advanced competitive weightlifters with 4+ years of continuous structured training
- Athletes with established, reliable technique in both competition lifts under ≥ 90% load
- Athletes with confirmed joint health (patellar tendon, shoulder girdle)
- **Not appropriate for:** intermediate athletes, athletes with any chronic joint pain, athletes without daily access to a platform and experienced coaching

## Frequency Ramp-Up

| Development Phase | Sessions/Day | Sessions/Week |
|------------------|-------------|---------------|
| Entry (months 1–3) | 1 | 6 |
| Intermediate (months 4–8) | 1–2 (MWF double) | 9 |
| Full Bulgarian (months 9+) | 2 (Mon–Sat) | 12 |

Do not advance to the next frequency tier until daily maxes are stable across 3+ consecutive weeks at the current tier.

## Single-Session Day Structure

| Order | Movement | Protocol |
|-------|----------|----------|
| 1 | Snatch | Build to daily max; reduce 10–15%, continue singles |
| 2 | Clean & Jerk | Build to daily max; reduce 10–15%, continue singles |
| 3 | Front Squat | Build to daily max |

## Double-Session Day Structure (AM / PM)

| Session | Movement | Protocol |
|---------|----------|----------|
| AM | Snatch | Daily max + back-off singles |
| AM | Front Squat | Daily max |
| PM | Clean & Jerk | Daily max + back-off singles |
| PM | Front Squat | Daily max |

## The Daily Max Defined

The daily max is the heaviest weight achievable that day where:
- Technique is indistinguishable from competition-standard execution
- No grinding, visible strain, or positional breakdown
- No stimulants or deliberate arousal manipulation

A daily max that is lower than yesterday is valid data. Chronic suppression of daily maxes over multiple consecutive sessions is the recovery signal; rest or reduce frequency.

## Key Rules

- **Zero accessory work.** No rows, no Romanian deadlifts, no overhead press. No back squat. The Bulgarian system treats any non-specific movement as adaptation energy wasted.
- **Technical integrity is absolute.** A lift requiring positional compromise does not count as the daily max. Lower the bar and try again.
- **No forced PRs.** Attempting weights above the genuine daily max through psychological arousal defeats the autoregulatory mechanism entirely.
