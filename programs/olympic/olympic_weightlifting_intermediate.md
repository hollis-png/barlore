# Olympic Weightlifting Intermediate Program

This 12-week program bridges the gap between learning the competition lifts and training toward genuine performance numbers. The defining challenge at the intermediate level is consistency: producing technically clean lifts at 85–95% of 1RM across multiple sessions per week, not just on good days. The program structures this around two types of sessions for each lift — one heavy day where the athlete works to a daily max, and one volume day where mechanics are reinforced at controlled intensities.

## Who This Is For

- Athletes who can perform the full snatch and clean & jerk with sound mechanics (no longer using power versions as primary movements)
- Snatch 1RM in the range of 60–90% bodyweight; C&J 1RM in the range of 75–110% bodyweight
- Has completed a beginner program or equivalent and is no longer making weekly PR improvements
- Not appropriate for: athletes still developing receiving position mechanics — complete the beginner program first

## Schedule (4 days/week)

| Day | Session | Primary Focus |
|-----|---------|---------------|
| Monday | Session A | Snatch heavy + Front Squat + Overhead Squat + Snatch Pull |
| Tuesday | Session B | C&J heavy + Back Squat + Clean Pull |
| Thursday | Session C | Power Snatch (diagnostic) + Snatch volume + Front Squat + Snatch Pull |
| Friday | Session D | C&J volume + Clean Pull + Front Squat |

Wednesday and the weekend are mandatory rest. The Tuesday-Thursday gap (one rest day between heavy and volume sessions) is intentional — neuromuscular readiness for volume work requires partial recovery from the heavy session.

## Session A — Snatch Heavy

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Intensity |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Overhead Squat | 3 × 3–4 | 60–70% snatch 1RM |
| Snatch | Build to daily max, then 2–3 back-off singles | Daily max; back-offs at 85% of daily max |
| Front Squat | 4 × 3 | 80–90% front squat 1RM |
| Snatch Pull | 4 × 3 | 95–105% snatch 1RM (Block 1 & 3); 110–120% (Block 2) |

Overhead squat opens every Session A — it is a positional check before loading the snatch, not a warm-up exercise. If overhead squat quality is noticeably lower than last week, reduce the snatch daily max target by 5%.

## Session B — Clean & Jerk Heavy

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Intensity |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Clean & Jerk | Build to daily max, then 2–3 back-off singles | Daily max; back-offs at 85% of daily max |
| Back Squat | 4 × 4–5 | 75–85% back squat 1RM |
| Clean Pull | 4 × 3 | 95–105% clean 1RM (Block 1 & 3); 110–120% (Block 2) |

## Session C — Snatch Volume

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Intensity |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Power Snatch | 3 × 3 | 75–80% snatch 1RM |
| Snatch | 5 × 2 | 78–85% snatch 1RM (block-dependent) |
| Hang Snatch (Block 2+) | 3 × 3 | 70–75% snatch 1RM |
| Front Squat | 3 × 4–5 | 70–80% front squat 1RM |
| Snatch Pull | 4 × 3 | 95–105% snatch 1RM (Block 1 & 3); 110–120% (Block 2) |

Power snatch is always performed before the full snatch. If bar path in the power snatch is inconsistent, reduce the full snatch volume sets from 5 × 2 to 3 × 2 and focus on position.

## Session D — C&J Volume

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Intensity |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Clean & Jerk | 4 × 1+1 | 80–88% C&J 1RM (block-dependent) |
| Clean Pull | 4 × 3 | 95–105% clean 1RM |
| Front Squat | 3 × 4–5 | 70–80% front squat 1RM |

The "1+1" notation means one clean followed by one jerk as a complex — not a split-second touch-and-jerk, but a deliberate pause in the rack position (3–5 seconds) before the jerk. This pause is training the rack reset and breath control that become critical at 90%+ loads.

## Block Structure

### Block 1 — Accumulation (Weeks 1–4)

Focus: technical consistency at moderate intensity; establish baseline daily max numbers.

| Session | Snatch/C&J working sets | Squats |
|---------|------------------------|--------|
| A / B (heavy) | Daily max target: 88–92% of 1RM | Front squat: 80–85% |
| C / D (volume) | 5 × 2 at 78–82% | Front squat: 70–75% |

Week 4 is a deload: all working weights reduced to 70–75%; daily max capped at 85% of 1RM; no attempts above this ceiling.

### Block 2 — Intensification (Weeks 5–8)

Focus: push daily max toward and occasionally past 1RM; introduce hang snatch in Session C. Pull intensity increases to 110–120% to maximize peak vertical ground reaction force and overload spinal extensors.

| Session | Snatch/C&J working sets | Squats |
|---------|------------------------|--------|
| A / B (heavy) | Daily max target: 92–97% of 1RM | Front squat: 85–90% |
| C / D (volume) | 5 × 2 at 82–86% | Front squat: 75–80% |
| Pulls (all sessions) | 4 × 3 at 110–120% of lift 1RM | — |

Begin hang snatch in Session C from Week 5. Update 1RM estimates based on best daily maxes from Block 1 before setting Block 2 percentages.

Week 8 is a deload: same protocol as Week 4.

### Block 3 — Peaking (Weeks 9–12)

Focus: consolidate heavy singles; attempt new 1RMs in week 11 or 12 test week.

| Session | Snatch/C&J working sets | Squats |
|---------|------------------------|--------|
| A / B (heavy) | Daily max target: 95–100%+ of 1RM | Front squat: 88–93% |
| C / D (volume) | Reduce to 3 × 2 at 82–86% | Front squat: 75–82% |

In Block 3, reduce volume day set count (3 × 2, not 5 × 2) to allow full recovery for heavy days. Week 12 can be structured as a mini-competition: attempt a new 1RM in snatch and C&J on separate days (snatch on Monday, C&J on Tuesday or Wednesday).

## Key Rules

- **RPE 9.5 ceiling on daily max:** The daily max is bounded by feel, not by a target number. Never attempt a weight that leaves less than 1 rep in reserve — if the last successful rep required grinding or significant positional breakdown, that is the daily max. True failure is prohibited.
- **Two-miss rule:** After two consecutive misses at the same weight, reduce by 3–5 kg and complete one successful lift before stopping that movement. Never end a session on a failed lift.
- **Back-off sets are mandatory:** After every daily max attempt, complete 2–3 back-off singles at 85% of the daily max reached. Skipping back-offs to protect the feeling of a heavy single is the most common error at this level.
- **Front squat priority:** If time or energy requires cutting one exercise, it is never front squat. Back squat, pulls, and accessory work are cut first.
- **Power snatch diagnostic:** If power snatch percentage of squat snatch exceeds 88%, the athlete is power-dependent and catch mechanics need dedicated attention before loads increase further.
