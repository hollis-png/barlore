# Glenn Pendlay Weightlifting System

Glenn Pendlay was the most influential American Olympic weightlifting coach of his generation, responsible for developing several national champions and international-caliber athletes. His system represents a structured Western adaptation of Eastern European high-frequency methods, modified for athletes who lack the multi-decade developmental foundation of Soviet-bloc lifters.

Pendlay's central insight was that American athletes at the intermediate level cannot tolerate the pure high-intensity, low-variability volume of the Bulgarian method. They need a **strength base** — specifically a raw leg and upper-back strength reserve that stays far ahead of the technical demands of the competition lifts. A lifter who can squat 200 kg but only clean 120 kg has enormous room for technical improvement without requiring more pulling strength. A lifter whose squat matches their clean will stall the moment their technique improves.

The program uses a **weekly Texas Method structure**: Volume Day drives the adaptation stimulus, Recovery Day allows fatigue clearance while maintaining technical exposure, and Intensity Day expresses the accumulated strength as a heavy single.

## Who This Is For

- Intermediate lifters who have established sound technique in the squat snatch and squat clean
- Athletes who have completed a beginner program and are training 4 days/week consistently
- Athletes whose squat strength has begun to limit their competition lift progress
- Not appropriate for: beginners who have not yet established squat receiving positions

## Schedule (4 days/week)

| Day | Session | Primary Stress |
|-----|---------|---------------|
| Monday | Volume Day | High-volume snatch, squat, pulls, rows |
| Tuesday | C&J Volume | Clean & Jerk volume + supplementary |
| Wednesday | Recovery Day | Light snatch, front squat, push press |
| Friday | Intensity Day | Heavy snatch + C&J singles, heavy squats |

Thursday and the weekend are rest.

## Monday — Volume Day

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Intensity |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Snatch | 5 × 3 | 72–78% |
| Back Squat | 5 × 5 | 75–80% |
| Snatch Pull | 4 × 3 | 100–105% snatch 1RM |
| Clean Pull | 4 × 3 | 100–105% clean 1RM |
| Bent-Over Row | 4 × 5 | Moderate; dead-stop each rep |

## Tuesday — C&J Volume

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Intensity |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Clean & Jerk | 4 × 2 | 72–78% |
| Front Squat | 3 × 4 | 70–75% |
| Push Press | 4 × 5 | Moderate |

## Wednesday — Recovery Day

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Intensity |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Snatch | 4 × 2 | 65–70% |
| Snatch Pull | 3 × 3 | 90–95% |
| Front Squat | 4 × 3 | 70–75% |
| Push Press | 3 × 5 | Light |
| Bent-Over Row | 3 × 5 | Light |

## Friday — Intensity Day

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Intensity |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Snatch | Build to heavy single; 2–3 back-offs | Daily max → 85% back-offs |
| Clean & Jerk | Build to heavy single; 2 back-offs | Daily max → 85% back-offs |
| Back Squat | 1 × 5 | 85–90% |
| Front Squat | 3 × 3 | 80–85% |
| Clean Pull | 3 × 3 | 105–110% |

## Key Rules

- **Squat first, lift second:** Never let the back squat stop progressing. If the competition lifts plateau while squats are still improving, the problem is technique. If squats plateau too, the strength base needs to be rebuilt.
- **Dead-stop rows every time:** The Pendlay Row is performed from the floor — no momentum, no stretch reflex. Each rep starts from a dead stop. This is the structural foundation of the upper back that protects intermediate lifters from spinal injury under heavy pulls.
- **Recovery Day is not optional:** The Wednesday session exists to allow fatigue clearance. Training it too hard turns the three-stress structure into a grind and removes the intensity expression on Friday.

> After this program, see `olympic_weightlifting_advanced` or `cal_strength_daily` for higher-frequency daily maximum training.
