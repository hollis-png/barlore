# Get Strong: The Ultimate 16-Week Transformation Program

*Get Strong* (2017) by Al and Danny Kavadlo is the Kavadlo brothers' first book to contain a complete, structured daily training program with specific sets, reps, progressions, warm-up guides, and rest days. Their earlier books (*Raising the Bar*, *Pushing the Limits!*) are exercise encyclopedias with step-by-step progression guides but without a complete program structure.

The defining feature of *Get Strong* is its **mandatory phase-end testing protocol**: at the end of each four-week phase, the athlete must meet a specific performance standard before advancing. If the standard is not met, the athlete repeats weeks 3–4 of the current phase and tests again. This enforces strict progression and prevents the premature advancement that is the most common failure mode in self-directed calisthenics programming.

## Who This Is For

- Beginners to intermediate athletes with some movement experience
- Athletes who want a structured, time-bound (16-week) calisthenics program with clear milestones
- Minimum baseline: 5 push-ups, 1 pull-up (band-assisted is acceptable for Phase 1)

## Four-Phase Structure

| Phase | Weeks | Theme | Primary Focus |
|-------|-------|-------|---------------|
| Phase 1 | 1–4 | Foundation | Movement quality; establish base reps; learn movement standards |
| Phase 2 | 5–8 | Brick and Mortar | Increase volume; introduce harder variations |
| Phase 3 | 9–12 | Concrete and Iron | Unilateral progressions; weighted options introduced |
| Phase 4 | 13–16 | Forged from Steel | Maximum volume and difficulty; peak capacity |

## 4-Day Weekly Schedule

*Get Strong* uses a 4-day schedule with 3 rest days. Recommended layout:

| Day | Focus |
|-----|-------|
| Day 1 | Upper push + Upper pull |
| Day 2 | Lower body + Core |
| Day 3 | Rest |
| Day 4 | Full body compound + Upper push |
| Day 5 | Rest |
| Day 6 | Full body + Core |
| Day 7 | Rest |

## End-of-Phase Testing (Mandatory Before Advancement)

| Phase Test | Minimum Standard |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Phase 1 → Phase 2 | 10 push-ups, 5 pull-ups, 10 air squats, 10 hanging knee raises |
| Phase 2 → Phase 3 | 20 push-ups, 8 pull-ups, 8 Bulgarian split squats per leg, 10 hanging bent-leg raises |
| Phase 3 → Phase 4 | 10 archer push-ups per side, 10 pull-ups, 5 box pistol squats per leg, 8 hanging straight-leg raises |

*If any element of the phase test is not passed, return to weeks 3–4 of the current phase and retest.*

## Key Rules

- **Phase tests are non-negotiable.** Advancing without passing the test means entering the next phase without the structural foundation that phase was designed to build. The Kavadlos designed the failure protocol (repeat weeks 3–4) because most athletes will pass on the second attempt without significant setback.
- **Add sets, not reps, when stuck.** If prescribed reps cannot be reached, add sets at a lower rep count rather than lowering reps per set. The cumulative volume per session is the adaptation driver.
- **Push-pull balance is built into the schedule.** Do not reorder sessions to front-load either push or pull days.
