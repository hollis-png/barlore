# Calisthenics Advanced Strength Skills

Advanced calisthenics targets static strength skills (planche, front lever, handstand) and elite dynamic skills (ring muscle-up, one-arm pull-up progressions). These movements require force production in positions where the body's lever arm against gravity is maximized — making them among the most demanding strength feats achievable with bodyweight. The training adaptation timeline is longer than any other program in this knowledge base: tendons, ligaments, and specific motor pathways for these positions require 12–24 months of consistent work, and the program reflects this by being 24 weeks.

## Who This Is For

- Athletes who have completed `calisthenics_intermediate_skills` or equivalent
- Minimum baselines: 5 strict ring muscle-ups, 15-second L-sit, freestanding handstand hold 5 s, weighted pull-up +30 kg
- Training age in calisthenics: 2+ years

## Skill Practice Format

Each skill session follows this structure:
1. **Activation**: scapular pull-ups + hollow body (5 min)
2. **Max quality reps**: 5–8 working sets of the skill progression at max quality, stopping before form degrades
3. **Volume sets**: 2–3 back-off sets at one step easier progression, higher reps

**Planche progression ladder** (each step requires 3 × 8 s with control):
Tuck (bent knee) → Advanced Tuck (knee to elbow) → Straddle → Full

**Front Lever progression ladder** (each step requires 3 × 8 s):
Tuck → Advanced Tuck → One-leg → Straddle → Full

**Handstand push-up ladder** (each step: 3 × 5 reps):
Elevated pike → Wall HSPU (head touches) → Wall HSPU (floor) → Freestanding negatives → Freestanding HSPU

## Connective Tissue Management

Planche and front lever training concentrate extraordinary force at the shoulder, elbow, and wrist in extended lever positions. These structures adapt more slowly than the muscles generating the force. Mandatory protocols:
- **Gelatin + Vitamin C** before every session (see `connective_tissue`)
- **Scapular pull-ups** every session without exception
- **Never train a skill to failure**: stop 2 reps before form breaks down
- If elbow or wrist pain develops: reduce progression difficulty by two steps immediately; do not train through tendon pain

Expect 3–6 months of building tolerance before working sets feel "normal" on planche and front lever progressions.
