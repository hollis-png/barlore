# Convict Conditioning Big Six

*Convict Conditioning* (2011) by Paul "Coach" Wade presents a minimalist bodyweight training system built around six fundamental human movement patterns, each with a ten-step progression from complete beginner to elite. The stated philosophy is that modern gym culture has abandoned the systematic progression logic of older physical culture, replacing it with machine isolation and external load that bypasses the body's own adaptive progression.

The system requires no equipment beyond a pull-up bar and a floor. The six movements — Push, Pull, Squat, Leg Raise, Bridge, and Handstand Push-up — are presented as the complete set of movements needed to develop "supreme strength." No isolation work, no machines, no additional accessories.

The most distinctive aspect of the system is its **deliberate pace**. Unlike programs that rush toward advanced movements, Convict Conditioning insists on mastering each step completely before advancement. The stated reason is connective tissue: tendons, ligaments, and joint capsules adapt far more slowly than muscle tissue, and skipping steps means arriving at the advanced progressions with musculature strong enough to produce the movement but connective tissue too underdeveloped to sustain the load safely.

## Who This Is For

- Absolute beginners with no prior training
- Athletes training without equipment (home, travel)
- Intermediate athletes seeking to develop advanced unilateral movements (one-arm pull-up, one-arm push-up, pistol squat)
- Anyone interested in the classic physical culture approach to bodyweight development

## Big Six Overview

| Series | Entry (Step 1) | Master Step (Step 10) |
|--------|---------------|----------------------|
| Push (horizontal) | Wall Push-up | One-Arm Push-up |
| Pull (vertical) | Jackknife Pull-up | One-Arm Pull-up |
| Squat (knee-dominant) | Shoulderstand Squat | Pistol Squat |
| Leg Raise (core) | Lying Knee Tuck | Hanging Windshield Wiper |
| Bridge (posterior chain) | Short Bridge | Stand-to-Stand Bridge |
| Handstand Push-up (overhead) | Wall Headstand Hold | One-Arm Handstand Push-up |

## Weekly Training Schedule (3-Day Rotation)

Convict Conditioning prescribes a **big-little** alternating schedule:

| Day | Series Trained |
|-----|---------------|
| Monday | Push + Pull |
| Wednesday | Legs (Squat + Leg Raise) |
| Friday | Bridge + Handstand Push-up |

All six series are trained three times per week on this schedule (each twice per week). The system also provides a more frequent 6-day schedule for athletes who want to train each series more often.

## Advancement Criteria (Consolidation Standards)

Advance to the next step only when the current consolidation standard is met for **three consecutive training sessions**:

| Step | Movement | Consolidation Standard |
|------|----------|----------------------|
| 1 | Wall Push-up | 3 × 50 |
| 5 | Full Push-up | 3 × 30 |
| 10 | One-Arm Push-up | 1 × 100 per arm |
| 5 | Full Pull-up | 3 × 15 |
| 10 | One-Arm Pull-up | 1 × 6 per arm |
| 5 | Full Squat | 3 × 50 |
| 10 | Pistol Squat | 2 × 50 per leg |
| 5 | Hanging Knee Raise | 3 × 30 |
| 8 | Hanging Straight Leg Raise | 3 × 20 |

## Key Rules

- **Connective tissue pace, not muscle pace.** The advancement criteria are high-rep standards deliberately chosen to ensure connective tissue has fully adapted before the next progression is introduced. Skipping a step because "it feels too easy" is the most common cause of injury at higher steps.
- **Never train on consecutive days.** The system's rest structure is not negotiable — 48 hours between training sessions is the minimum recovery window.
- **Each step is an independent milestone.** The six series progress independently — being at Step 8 in Pull-ups while at Step 3 in Handstand Push-ups is normal and expected. Do not artificially synchronize steps.
