# German Volume Training (GVT)

German Volume Training is attributed to Charles Poliquin, who popularized the 10×10 protocol as a hypertrophy shock method that German national weightlifters used during the 1970s off-season to gain muscle mass rapidly. The protocol's power derives from an unusual combination: extremely high per-session volume (100 reps per muscle group) at a moderate intensity (60% 1RM) — a zone that generates maximal metabolic stress and muscle damage while remaining technically manageable across all 10 sets.

## Who This Is For

- Intermediate bodybuilders who have hit a hypertrophy plateau on their current program and need a volume-shock stimulus
- Athletes transitioning between strength blocks who want a dedicated hypertrophy phase (6 weeks)
- Note: GVT is not a long-term program — 6 weeks is the intended maximum; strength temporarily decreases under this volume load and must be rebuilt afterward

## The 10×10 Protocol Rules

1. **Load**: 60% of 1RM — this is approximately a 20RM load, which means the first 5 sets feel easy
2. **Rep tempo**: 4-0-2-0 (4 seconds down, 0 pause, 2 seconds up, 0 pause)
3. **Rest between antagonist pairs**: 90 seconds (from end of A1 to start of A2, and from end of A2 to start of A1)
4. **Load does not change** across all 10 sets — even if you fail, reduce rest to 60 seconds but keep the load
5. **When 10×10 is completed clean**: increase load by 2.5% at the next session
6. **Accessory work**: maximum 2–3 sets of 2 exercises; GVT volume is sufficient stimulus

## What to Expect

- **Week 1**: Sets 1–5 feel too easy; sets 8–10 become genuinely hard
- **Week 2**: Full-body DOMS especially after leg day; performance temporarily decreases
- **Week 3**: Adaptation begins; able to complete all 10×10 with control
- **Weeks 4–6**: Progressive small load increases become possible
- **After GVT**: Take 2–4 weeks of lower-volume, higher-intensity work to rebuild strength before running again
