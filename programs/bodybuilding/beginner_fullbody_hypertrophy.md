# Beginner Full-Body Hypertrophy

A three-day-per-week full-body program designed for trainees with less than 6–12 months of consistent resistance training. Every session trains every major muscle group, which maximizes weekly frequency while keeping per-session volume manageable. Beginners adapt primarily through neural mechanisms in the first 4–8 weeks and accumulate muscle mass from 8–12 weeks onward; training each pattern three times per week accelerates motor learning for the compound movements that form the long-term foundation.

## Who This Is For

- Trainees with < 1 year of consistent barbell/dumbbell training
- Anyone returning after a layoff > 6 months
- Trainees transitioning from machine-based training to free weights
- Those who cannot commit to 4–6 day splits

**Not appropriate for**: intermediate trainees who have stalled on beginner linear progression (use PHUL, PPL, or RP Hypertrophy instead).

## Schedule

Sessions rotate A/B every training day:

| Week | Mon | Wed | Fri |
|------|-----|-----|-----|
| 1 | A | B | A |
| 2 | B | A | B |
| 3 | A | B | A |
| … | … | … | … |

Rest at least one day between sessions. Weekend rest is flexible — Mon/Wed/Fri is conventional but Tue/Thu/Sat works equally well.

## Session A

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| Back Squat | 3 × 6–10 | Primary lower-body compound |
| Bench Press | 3 × 8–12 | Primary horizontal push |
| Bent-over Row | 3 × 8–12 | Primary horizontal pull |
| Romanian Deadlift | 3 × 10–12 | Posterior chain |
| Barbell Curl | 3 × 10–15 | Elbow flexors |
| Triceps Pushdown | 3 × 12–15 | Triceps |
| Side Lateral Raise | 3 × 15–20 | Lateral deltoid |

**Session A duration**: 55–70 minutes including warm-up sets.

## Session B

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| Leg Press | 3 × 10–15 | Secondary quad compound |
| Incline Dumbbell Press | 3 × 10–12 | Upper chest angle variation |
| Pull-ups | 3 × 5–8 (or to failure) | Primary vertical pull |
| Overhead Press | 3 × 8–12 | Vertical push compound |
| Hammer Curls | 3 × 12–15 | Brachialis and brachioradialis |
| Lying Triceps Press | 3 × 12–15 | Triceps long head |
| Face Pull | 3 × 15–20 | Rear delt / shoulder health |

**Session B duration**: 55–70 minutes including warm-up sets.

## Warm-Up Protocol

Before each session:
1. 5 min light cardio (row, bike, or brisk walk)
2. For the first compound movement only, ramp up: 50% × 8, 70% × 5, 85% × 2, then working sets
3. Subsequent exercises need only 1 light warm-up set

## Progression

**Linear double-progression**:
1. Start every exercise at the bottom of its rep range with a weight that leaves 3–4 reps in reserve (RIR) on the last set
2. Each session: attempt to beat the previous session's rep count at the same weight
3. When the top of the rep range is achieved for all sets: add weight next session, reset to bottom of range
4. If minimum reps cannot be completed: hold weight, try again next session
5. After two failed sessions at the same weight: reduce load by 10%, rebuild

**Example — Bench Press at 60 kg**:
- Session 1: 3 × 8 reps → goal is to hit 8, 8, 8
- Session 3: 60 kg × 8, 8, 10 → still below top (12); continue at 60 kg
- Session 5: 60 kg × 10, 11, 12 → all sets hit top of range → next session: 62.5 kg × 8, 8, 8

Beginners typically progress every session or every other session on compound lifts. This rate slows after 8–12 weeks.

## Pull-up Scaling

If fewer than 5 unassisted pull-ups can be performed:
- Use a resistance band looped around the bar (reduces effective bodyweight)
- Or substitute lat pulldown at equivalent load
- Progress by gradually reducing band resistance or increasing lat pulldown weight

Do not use kipping or momentum — full range, controlled tempo.

## When to Move On

Progress to an intermediate program (PHUL, PPL, or RP Hypertrophy) when:
- Linear progression on compounds stalls for 2–3 consecutive weeks despite adequate sleep and nutrition
- Approximately 12 weeks of consistent training at this program
- Squat ≥ 1× bodyweight, Bench ≥ 0.7× bodyweight, Row ≥ 0.75× bodyweight (rough benchmarks)
