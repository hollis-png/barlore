# Bro Split (5-Day Body-Part Split)

The bro split — one muscle group per day across five sessions — is the most derided and most practiced training structure in bodybuilding history simultaneously. Its central trade-off is undeniable: each muscle group is hit only once per week, which modern meta-analyses show is suboptimal for frequency, but the per-session volume concentration generates intense metabolic stress, muscle damage, and the classic "pump" that practitioners report produces faster hypertrophic gains than they experience with higher-frequency programs. The research verdict is that twice-per-week frequency is superior on average, but the bro split remains valuable for intermediate athletes with high per-session recovery capacity, limited weekly schedule flexibility, or the need for a psychological break from technically demanding programs.

## Who This Is For

- Intermediate trainees (1–2 years of consistent training) who have completed full-body or upper-lower programs and want to increase per-session volume dramatically
- Those who can only train at consistent times on 5 weekdays and cannot easily rearrange their schedule to optimize 48-hour muscle recovery windows
- Bodybuilders entering a hypertrophy specialization phase who want to experience maximum per-session stimulus on each muscle group

## Progression

- **Compound lifts**: Linear load increase when all prescribed reps completed with good form at the top of the rep range
- **Isolation lifts**: Double-progression — advance reps within the given range, then add load (2.5 kg) at the next session
- **Stalling**: If load progress stalls for 2 consecutive sessions on a compound lift, reduce load 10% and build back up
- **Deload (Weeks 9 and optionally 5)**: Reduce to 60% of total working sets for one week at maintained loads

## Frequency Trade-off

The evidence-based critique of the bro split is valid: once-per-week frequency per muscle group produces less hypertrophy per unit of training time than twice-per-week in most meta-analyses. However, the bro split has practical advantages:

1. Each session is laser-focused — the trainee knows exactly what they are training and why
2. High per-session volume provides maximal metabolic stress on the target muscle
3. Weekly structure is simple to follow without needing to track muscle recovery windows
4. Many experienced intermediate athletes report superior results on the bro split compared to lower-volume, higher-frequency approaches — likely due to individual recovery differences

The bro split works best for athletes who prioritize pump-based training, have high caloric intake and recovery support, or have previously plateaued on higher-frequency programs.
