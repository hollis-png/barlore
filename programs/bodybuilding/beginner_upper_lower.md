# Beginner Upper/Lower Split

A four-day-per-week program that splits training into upper body and lower body sessions, each performed twice per week. This structure provides more per-session volume and exercise variety than the three-day full-body approach while maintaining the twice-weekly frequency per muscle group that Schoenfeld et al. (2016, meta-analysis) found optimal for hypertrophy.

## Who This Is For

- Trainees with < 1 year of consistent training who can commit to 4 days per week
- Beginners who find full-body sessions too long or fatiguing (3-day full-body programs compress 14+ exercises into 3 sessions)
- Anyone who prefers a more focused session: fewer movement patterns per day, more attention to each
- Trainees who want a structured stepping stone toward intermediate splits (PPL, PHUL)

**Not appropriate for**: trainees who can only train 3 days per week (use Beginner Full-Body Hypertrophy instead) or intermediate trainees who have exhausted beginner linear progression.

## Schedule

| Day | Session | Focus |
|-----|---------|-------|
| Monday | Upper A | Press-dominant (bench, OHP, row, pull-up) |
| Tuesday | Lower A | Squat-dominant (back squat, RDL, leg press) |
| Wednesday | Rest | |
| Thursday | Upper B | Press-variation (incline DB, cable row, face pull) |
| Friday | Lower B | Hinge-dominant (deadlift, front squat, leg curl) |
| Sat–Sun | Rest | |

The day labels are flexible — any arrangement with at least one rest day between same-type sessions works (e.g., Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri, Tue/Wed/Fri/Sat).

## Warm-Up Protocol

Before each session:
1. 5 min light cardio (row, bike, or brisk walk)
2. For the first compound movement only, ramp up: 50% × 8, 70% × 5, 85% × 2, then working sets
3. Subsequent compounds need only 1 light warm-up set; isolation exercises need no warm-up

## Progression

**Linear double-progression** (identical to Beginner Full-Body Hypertrophy):
1. Start every exercise at the bottom of its rep range with a weight that leaves 3–4 RIR on the last set
2. Each session: attempt to beat the previous session's rep count at the same weight
3. When all sets hit the top of the rep range: add weight next session, reset to bottom of range
4. If minimum reps cannot be completed: hold weight, try again next session
5. After two failed sessions at the same weight: reduce load by 10%, rebuild

**Weight increments**:
- Upper body compounds (bench, OHP, row): +2.5 kg
- Lower body compounds (squat, deadlift): +5 kg
- Isolation exercises: +1.25–2.5 kg (or add 1–2 reps per set before increasing weight)

## Why Upper/Lower Instead of Full-Body

Both structures work for beginners. The choice depends on schedule and preference:

| Factor | Full-Body 3x | Upper/Lower 4x |
|--------|-------------|----------------|
| Days per week | 3 | 4 |
| Exercises per session | 7 | 4–7 |
| Session duration | 55–70 min | 40–65 min |
| Volume per muscle/week | Equal | Equal (distributed across 2 sessions) |
| Fatigue management | Higher per-session (everything in one day) | Lower per-session (split across days) |
| Exercise variety | Lower (same exercises every session) | Higher (A/B variation within each split) |

## When to Move On

Progress to an intermediate program (PHUL, PPL, RP Hypertrophy) when:
- Linear progression on compounds stalls for 2–3 consecutive weeks despite adequate sleep and nutrition
- Approximately 12 weeks of consistent training at this program
- Squat ≥ 1× bodyweight, Bench ≥ 0.7× bodyweight, Deadlift ≥ 1.25× bodyweight (rough benchmarks)
