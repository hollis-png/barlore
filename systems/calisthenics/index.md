# Calisthenics

Calisthenics is the practice of building strength, skill, and physique using bodyweight movements — from foundational pulls and pushes to advanced static holds (planche, front lever, human flag) and dynamic skills (muscle-up, handstand). Equipment is minimal: a pull-up bar, parallel bars, rings, and floor space are sufficient for a complete training system.

## Defining Principles

- **Skill progression over load progression**: Without an external barbell to add weight to, difficulty is increased by progressing to harder movement variants (e.g., pike push-up → handstand push-up) or by adjusting leverage (e.g., incline row → horizontal row → front lever row).
- **Straight-arm vs. bent-arm strength**: Calisthenics uniquely trains straight-arm strength (planche, front lever, back lever) which places exceptional demands on the shoulder girdle tendons and connective tissue. Bent-arm strength (pull-ups, dips, push-ups) is the more familiar domain shared with gym training.
- **Isometric foundations**: Many advanced skills are primarily isometric holds. Building the hold time and positional quality is the primary work; dynamic movements come after the position is stable.
- **Long-term tendon adaptation**: Connective tissue adapts much more slowly than muscle. Calisthenics programming must be conservative with loading and progression timelines to avoid tendon injury in the wrists, elbows, and shoulders.
- **Movement quality as the metric**: A strict muscle-up is categorically different from a kipping one. Form standards are enforced as part of the skill definition.

## What Distinguishes Calisthenics from Other Systems

Calisthenics is the only major strength system where load progression requires advancing to structurally harder movements rather than adding weight. This demands a different approach to programming — skill trees replace rep maxes as the primary progress tracking tool. Unlike gymnastics, competition is informal; unlike powerlifting, there is no standardized meet format in most calisthenics contexts.
