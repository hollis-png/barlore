# Powerlifting

Powerlifting is the sport of lifting the maximum possible load for one repetition in three competition lifts: the squat, bench press, and deadlift. Every training decision — exercise selection, technique, periodization, and volume distribution — is subordinated to one goal: moving more weight on the platform.

## Defining Principles

- **Specificity**: Training centers almost exclusively on the three competition lifts and their close variants. Accessory work serves the competition lifts, not general fitness.
- **Progressive overload through load**: The primary progression variable is the weight on the bar, not volume or exercise variety.
- **Technique for leverage**: Movement patterns are optimized for mechanical advantage (shorter moment arms, tighter leverages), not muscle isolation.
- **Periodization to a peak**: Training cycles are organized backward from a competition date, moving from high-volume/lower-intensity accumulation toward low-volume/maximal-intensity peaking.
- **Low rep, high intensity**: Working sets cluster in the 1–5 rep range at 75–95% of 1RM for the main lifts.

## What Distinguishes Powerlifting from Other Systems

Unlike bodybuilding, muscle hypertrophy is a means to an end (more muscle cross-section → more force production), not a goal in itself. Unlike weightlifting, there is no ballistic or overhead component — all three lifts are grinding, slow-force expressions. Unlike strongman, equipment and movements are standardized and performed individually.

## Athlete Profile

Powerlifting suits athletes who:
- Are motivated by objective, measurable performance (the weight moved is the score)
- Prefer low-variety, high-specificity training over novelty
- Have the patience for slow, multi-month strength adaptation cycles
- Are willing to learn precise technique on three movements and refine it for years

**Not ideal for:** athletes whose primary goal is aesthetic physique development, general fitness, or sport-specific conditioning.

## Core Exercises

| Movement | Role | Key variants |
|----------|------|--------------|
| Back squat | Competition lift | Low-bar, high-bar, box squat, pause squat |
| Bench press | Competition lift | Close-grip, pause bench, board press, floor press |
| Conventional deadlift | Competition lift | Sumo, deficit, Romanian, rack pull |
| Overhead press | Accessory | Strict press, push press (shoulder health) |
| Row variations | Accessory | Barbell row, Pendlay row (upper back for bench/deadlift) |

## Typical Training Parameters

| Variable | Novice | Intermediate | Advanced |
|----------|--------|--------------|---------|
| Intensity (main lifts) | 60-80% 1RM | 70-90% 1RM | 75-97% 1RM |
| Weekly sets per lift | 10-15 | 15-25 | 20-35 |
| Rep range (main lifts) | 5s | 1-5 | 1-3 |
| Sessions per week | 3 | 3-4 | 4-6 |
| Periodization | Linear | Weekly undulating / Texas Method | Block / conjugate |

## Progression Pathway

**Novice (0-12 months):** Linear progression — add weight each session. `starting_strength` is the canonical template. Session frequency: 3x/week on the three lifts.

**Early intermediate (1-2 years):** Weekly progression — volume day / intensity day structure. `texas_method` or `gzclp` extends linear gains. Begin meet preparation.

**Intermediate (2-4 years):** Monthly progression — block periodization, percentage-based wave loading. `5_3_1` is the dominant framework. First competition typically occurs here.

**Advanced (4+ years):** Peaking cycles measured in months. `westside_conjugate` (max-effort / dynamic-effort conjugation) or competition-specific blocks with coach-designed peaking.

## Competition Format

| Federation | Tested? | Equipment | Notable rules |
|------------|---------|-----------|---------------|
| IPF / USAPL | Yes (WADA) | Raw / equipped | Strictest technique judging |
| USPA | Optional | Raw / equipped | Allows knee wraps in raw division |
| RPS / SPF | No | Raw / equipped | More permissive equipment rules |

Competition day: each lifter gets 3 attempts per lift. Best successful attempt in each lift totals the score. Weight classes range from 52 kg to 120+ kg.
