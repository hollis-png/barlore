# CrossFit

CrossFit defines fitness as work capacity across broad time and modal domains. Training combines barbell strength, Olympic lifting, gymnastics, and metabolic conditioning (monostructural cardio) into varied, high-intensity workouts. The goal is not to be the best at any single physical quality but to be highly capable across all of them.

## Defining Principles

- **Variance**: Workouts are intentionally varied so that no single adaptation is overemphasized and no movement becomes a crutch. The athlete who trains everything is fitter than the specialist.
- **Constant high intensity**: The defining feature of most CrossFit workouts (WODs) is that they are performed at or near maximum sustainable intensity. Time and reps are scored, creating competitive accountability for effort.
- **Functional movement patterns**: CrossFit selects movements that are universal motor recruitment patterns — squat, hinge, press, pull, carry, and their loaded gymnastics equivalents. Machines and isolation exercises are largely absent.
- **Ten physical domains**: Training aims to develop cardiovascular endurance, stamina, strength, flexibility, power, speed, coordination, agility, balance, and accuracy simultaneously.
- **Metabolic conditioning as a first-class citizen**: Unlike pure strength sports, conditioning (running, rowing, cycling, jump rope) is not an afterthought — it is integrated into every training session alongside strength and skill work.

## What Distinguishes CrossFit from Other Systems

CrossFit is the only major training system that explicitly programs for work capacity across all time domains (seconds to 30+ minutes) within a single training session and training week. Unlike powerlifting or weightlifting, there is no fixed competition lift set; unlike bodybuilding, appearance is not the goal; unlike calisthenics, external load is central to the methodology. The combination of Olympic lifting, gymnastics, and metabolic conditioning into a single session is unique to CrossFit and its derivatives.
