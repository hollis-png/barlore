# Bodybuilding Lens: Back Squat

In bodybuilding the goal of the back squat is not to move maximal load but to maximize mechanical tension and metabolic stress in the quads and glutes to drive hypertrophy.

## Technical Approach

Bodybuilders generally use a **high bar position**: a more upright torso, quad-dominant, stimulating the target muscles more directly. Stance is narrower and depth often pursues full range of motion (ATG) to lengthen the time the muscle spends under tension. Compared with powerlifting, bar weight is not the priority — movement quality and target-muscle connection are.

## Intensity Ranges

| Goal | Intensity (%1RM) | Sets × Reps |
|------|------------------|-------------|
| Mechanical tension | 70–80% | 3–4 × 6–10 |
| Metabolic stress | 60–70% | 3–4 × 12–20 |
| Strength base | 80–85% | 3–4 × 5–8 |

## Volume Considerations

Bodybuilding uses weekly volume (effective sets per muscle per week) as the primary progression variable. The back squat is usually the main dish on leg day, supported by leg press, leg extensions, and similar to accumulate total volume. See `core/progressive_overload.md` for the volume principle.

## Common Trade-offs

- Sacrificing bar weight for the mind-muscle connection is acceptable
- Training close to failure (RIR 0–3) is more common than in powerlifting
- Full range of motion takes priority over load
