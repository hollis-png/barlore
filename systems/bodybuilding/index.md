# Bodybuilding

Bodybuilding trains for maximum muscle size, symmetry, and definition rather than performance. The sport is judged subjectively on appearance, which means training is organized to accumulate sufficient stimulus per muscle group to drive hypertrophy, while managing fatigue and maintaining balance across the physique.

## Defining Principles

- **Volume as the primary driver**: Weekly effective sets per muscle group is the key variable — more sets (within recovery capacity) means more growth signal.
- **Proximity to failure**: Most working sets are taken close to muscular failure (0–3 RIR), because mechanical tension is highest in that zone.
- **Exercise selection by target muscle**: Movements are chosen for their ability to stimulate a specific muscle, not for how much weight can be moved. A leg press that clearly activates the quads is more valuable than a squat that loads the whole system.
- **Full range of motion**: Lengthening a muscle under load and training it across its full range produces greater hypertrophic stimulus than partial-range work.
- **Specialization blocks**: Training can be organized to emphasize lagging muscle groups while maintaining others, cycling the focus over months.

## What Distinguishes Bodybuilding from Other Systems

Unlike powerlifting, load on the bar is not the progress metric — muscle size and proportion are. Unlike weightlifting, there is no skill component or ballistic demand. Unlike CrossFit, work capacity and metabolic conditioning are secondary; fatigue is managed, not maximized.

## Athlete Profile

Bodybuilding suits athletes who:
- Are motivated by visual progress — size, symmetry, definition — rather than performance metrics
- Enjoy exercise variety and are willing to learn which movements best stimulate each muscle
- Can commit to consistent nutrition tracking alongside training
- Have the patience for slow hypertrophic adaptation (0.5–1 kg lean mass/month in early stages)

**Not ideal for:** athletes whose primary goal is strength performance, athletic speed-power, or general conditioning.

## Core Exercises

| Muscle group | Primary compound | Key isolation |
|---|---|---|
| Chest | Bench press, incline press | Cable fly, pec deck |
| Back (width) | Pull-up, lat pulldown | Straight-arm pulldown |
| Back (thickness) | Barbell row, seated cable row | Face pull |
| Shoulders | Overhead press | Lateral raise, rear delt fly |
| Quads | Squat, leg press | Leg extension |
| Hamstrings | Romanian deadlift, leg curl | Nordic curl |
| Glutes | Hip thrust, Bulgarian split squat | Cable kickback |
| Biceps | Barbell curl | Incline dumbbell curl |
| Triceps | Close-grip bench, dips | Pushdown, overhead extension |

## Typical Training Parameters

| Variable | Accumulation | Intensification | Deload |
|---|---|---|---|
| RIR (proximity to failure) | 3-4 RIR | 0-2 RIR | 4-5 RIR |
| Weekly sets per muscle | MEV+2 → MAV | MAV → MRV | MV |
| Rep range | 8-20 | 6-15 | 10-20 |
| Sessions per week | 3-5 | 4-6 | 3 |
| Deload frequency | Every 4-6 weeks | — | 1 week |

Volume landmarks (RP framework): MV < MEV < MAV < MRV. See `core/volume_landmarks.md`.

## Progression Pathway

**Beginner (0-18 months):** Any structured program produces rapid gains. Full-body 3x/week or upper/lower 4x/week. Focus on compound movements and learning muscle-mind connection. `push_pull_legs` or `phul` are appropriate entry points.

**Intermediate (18 months - 4 years):** Gains slow — 1-2 kg/year lean mass. Volume must increase deliberately. `rp_hypertrophy` introduces autoregulated volume progression and mesocycle planning. Specialization blocks for lagging groups become relevant.

**Advanced (4+ years):** Annual lean mass gains <0.5 kg. Detailed periodization, peak week manipulation for competition, and precise nutrition periodization dominate. Off-season / prep cycle structure with coach support.
